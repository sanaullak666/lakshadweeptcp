notified for this Union Territory. The undersigned had no part in framing the draft Rules

2. The draft Rules govern the Planning and Development Authorities. These Authorities 
prepare the land use map and register, the Master Plan and town planning schemes for

re-roofing; levy development charges; and evict occupants (Sections 26, 29, 32, 48, 90

recorded, charged, regulated if notified as such .

Kerala dated 05.08.2026 (2026 KLT OnLine 2085). 
Summary of Objections 
The draft Rules should not be notified in their present form. Several provisions exceed the 
rule-making power in Section 153, it also  contradict the sections they claim to implement and 
several rules  are arbitrary infringing Articles 14, 21 and 300A of the Constitution of India.  
(i) Consultation is defective as the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes and 
the elected Panchayats were not consulted.

accepted only by email.

Secretaries on 10.01.2014, applies to subordinat e legislation. It requires a brief 
justification, the essential elements, the financial implications and an estimate of 
the impact on the environment, fundamental rights, and the lives and livelihoods of 
the people affected (paragraph 2). It requires publi city that reaches the affected

5. The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes and the elected Panchayats were 
not consulted.  About 95% of the people of Lakshadweep are Scheduled Tribes 
(Census 2011). Rules that decide who sits on the planning bodies, how tribal land is 
recorded and charged, and how occupants are evicted are " major policy matters 
affecting Scheduled Tribes", on which Article 338A(9) requires the Commission 
to be consulted. Part IX of the Constitution applies to Union Territories (Article 243L); 
the Gram Panchayats are the "local authority" under Section 2(18); and land 
improvement, land consolidation and rural ho using are Eleventh Schedule subjects 
(items 2 and 10). The District Panchayat, all ten Village (Dweep) Panchayats and the 
Commission should be consulted before the draft Rules are finalised. 
6. The draft Rules ignore the Pandaram judgment.  By the Pandaram jud gment, the 
High Court of Kerala held that persons in occupation of Pandaram land before 1965, 
and their legal heirs, are lawful owners. It held that the Administration had no power to 
treat such land as Government land and evict the occupants, and directed  occupancy 
certificates to be issued. The draft Rules do not refer to it. The land use register (Form 
A) has no column for owner or tenure, and Rule 21 allows eviction of anyone the 
Authority thinks is in "unauthorized occupation". The Rules should contain  an express 
saving clause: nothing in them shall be used to treat as unauthorised, or to evict, 
an occupant whose title or occupancy is protected by the Pandaram judgment, by 
the Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands (Protection of Scheduled Tribes) 
Regulation, 1964, or by the land revenue and tenancy law of the islands. 
7. The ecological safeguards of the Regulation are given no effect.  The Regulation 
requires carrying capacity studies (Sections 8(2)(j) and 26(1)(i)); zoning suited to islands 
four to six metres above mean sea level, backed by soil and geotechnical investigation 
(Section 33(1)(b)); and statutory clearances and conformity with the Integrated Island 
Management Plan (Section 33(1)(a)(ix) and (d)). The said  Plan was prepared under the 
directions of the Supreme Court in Union Territory of Lakshadweep v. Seashells Beach 
Resort (Civil Appeal Nos. 4625 –4626 of 2012). The Island Coastal Regulation Zone 
Notification, 2019 and the EIA Notification, 2006 also apply. The draft Rules give none 
of these requirements a form, a step or a check.

Objections to specific rules 
I. Membership, meetings and staff (Rules 3 to 10) 
9. Under Section 24(3), an Authority consists of a chairperson appointed by the 
Administrator, the Chief Executive Officer of the District Panchayat (ex officio), a Town 
Planning Officer appointed by the Administrator as Member Secretary, representatives 
nominated by the Grama Panchayats from among their members, and two experts co -
opted by the others. The Grama Panchayath nominees are the only elected voice. The 
draft Rules make their seats removable at will. 
10. Rule 3(i) – termination at will, and a term for ex officio members (ultra vires 
Sections 24(3)(d), 25(1) and 25(3)). The bracketed words "[Unless the term of office is 
terminated earlier by the Administrator]" arbitrary allow  the Administrator end any 
member's term without a ground, a notice or a hearing. Section 25(1) and Section 
153(2)(viii) allow the Rules to fix "the term of office and the conditions of service", not to 
make office terminable at pleasure. The words also contradict Rule 3(ii), which at least 
requires a show -cause notice. Rule 3(i) applies to "the Chairperson and members", 
which by Rule 2(v) includes the Town Planning Officer. Sectio n 25(1) and Section 
153(2)(viii) expressly exclude the Town Planning Officer, and an ex officio member's 
seat follows the office, not a term. The proviso then lets the Administrator extend terms 
to one year at a time, without limit.

12. Rule 5 – every casual vacancy filled by the Government (contrary to Sections 
24(3)(e) and 25(3)). Section 25(3) provides that a vacancy is filled "by fresh appointment 
by the Government, or by nomination by the local authority concerned, as the case may 
be". The experts are co-opted under Section 24(3)(e). Rule 5 says every vacancy "shall

Amendment sought: reproduce Section 25(3): a Gram Panchayat seat to be filled

13. Rule 3(iv) – conflict of interest.  The proviso exempts shareholders from the duty to 
disclose an interest. An Authority is a body corporate that can acquire, hold and dispose 
of land and enter into contracts (Section 24(2)), and can sell up to 15% of scheme land 
for residential, commercial or industrial use (Section 60(4)(k)(i)(D)). A member holding 
shares in a resort or real estate company concerned in a proposal is "not deemed" 
interested. 
Amendment sought: delete the shareholder proviso; extend the disclosure duty 
to interests of relatives and to persons associated under Rule 7; publish a register 
of interests. 
14. Rule 4 – a chairperson dependent on the Administrator.  The chairperson receives 
an honorarium of Rs. 500 a month. Other terms are those "specified in his order of 
appointment", and a Government servant chairperson serves on terms the Government 
sets "from time to time". The draft Rules prescribe no qualification for the post. With Rule 
3(i), the chairperson is in practice a serving officer holding office at the Administrator's 
pleasure. 
Amendment sought: qualifications for the chairperson (standing in public life or 
planning, residence in the planning area), a fixed term, and removal only after 
inquiry.

II. Land use, plans, permissions, charges and eviction (Rules 11 to 25) 
19. Rule 11 and Form A –. This record matters more than any other. Once the map and 
register are published in the Gazette, that publication is "conclusive proof that the map 
and register have been duly prepared and adopted" (Section 30(6)). From the Master 
Plan notice, every land use must conform (Section 46). Land shown as built-up or vacant 
is the natural target of town planning and land pooling schemes (Sections 33(2)(c) and 
60(3)). 
• Mainland categories.  The table lists Extensive, Heavy and Noxious Industries, 
Broadcasting Stations, Gas Works, Cinemas, Opera Houses, Market Gardens, 
Staple Crops, and Grazing Lan d and Pastures. Heavy and noxious industry has 
no place on coral islands governed by the Island Coastal Regulation Zone 
Notification, 2019 and the Integrated Island Management Plan, and its presence 
in the classification invites future zoning. 
• There is no category for coconut cultivation, the main land use and livelihood of 
the islands. Nor is there one for family homestead (tharavad) compounds, or for 
Pandaram holdings. Customary commons are missing: boat landing and hauling 
grounds, net-mending and fish-drying yards, and coconut-drying yards (kalam). So 
are mosques, dargahs and burial grounds as community assets. Beaches, 
lagoons, reefs, sand dunes and fresh-water recharge areas are absent, though the 
Regulation itself requires them to be identified (Secti ons 11(2)(f), (g) and (i) and 
33(1)(a)(ix) and (xiv)). "Vacant" is defined only as "partly built but unoccupied". 
• Form A records land use but not land rights. Its columns are survey number, area, 
sub-division, predominant use, number, storeys, age, type an d condition of 
structures, water, electricity and remarks. There is no column for owner or 
occupier, tenure (private, Pandaram or Government), basis of occupation, coastal 
regulation zone category, or customary rights of others such as access to the 
shore. The register will capture use while erasing who holds the land and by what 
right, which are exactly the facts the Pandaram judgment protects.

24. Rule 19 – a 90-day bar that defeats Section 52(1) (ultra vires). Section 52(1) gives a 
compensation claim to an owner who " had not served an acquisition notice under 
section 51, or if he had served such notice and the same had not been confirmed". 
An acquisition notice may be served within three months of the appeal order (Section 
51(1)), and the Government has up to a year to decide it (Section 51(5)). Rule 19 
requires the compensation claim within 90 days of the appeal order. An owner whose 
acquisition notice is refused after, say, eight months is already time-barred, because the 
time ran out while the Government was deciding. A rule cannot take away a right the 
Regulation gives. The time for refusing an offer of compensation under Section 52(4) 
(Section 153(2)(xxviii)) is not prescribed at all. 
Amendment sought: 90 days from the appeal order, or from the order refusing to 
confirm the acquisition notice, whichever is later; and a prescribed period under 
Section 52(4).

26. Rule 21 – summary eviction from "any land" (ultra vires Section 90; violates 
Articles 14, 21 and 300A). 
• Beyond Section 90.  Section 90 provides that, once a preliminary town planning 
scheme comes into force, "any person continuing to occupy any land which he is 
not entitled to occupy under the preliminary scheme" shall be summarily evicted 
"in accordance with such procedure, as may be prescribed". Section 153(2)(lvi) 
authorises only that procedure. Rule 21(1) instead applies whenever the Authority 
"is of the opinion that any person is in unauthorized occupation of any land", with 
no reference to a preliminary scheme. It creates a free -standing power to evict 
from any land in the planning area, including lan d the Administration claims as its 
own, such as Pandaram land. A rule cannot create a power the Regulation 
withholds. 
• Unfair procedure.  Rule 21 gives ten days to show cause. It allows service by 
affixing a copy at the "last known place of public resort in the village", which is

Page 11 of 16 
weaker than Section 126(1)(c), which requires tender, affixing at the residence or 
on the land, or registered post. Rule 21(4) excuses errors in the name of the person 
or the description of the land. The decision is taken by the Auth ority in which the 
land vests (Section 88(a)), so it judges its own cause. There is no appeal, unlike 
Sections 56(2), 57(2), 58(2) and 139. After 30 days the Authority may use "such 
force as may be necessary", with no protection for women, children or the elderly, 
no bar during the monsoon when sea travel stops, no inventory of belongings, and 
no rehabilitation. 
• Contrary to the Regulation's own guarantees.  Relocation is "without prejudice to 
the right to property" (Section 2(31)), as are the laying out of land and removal of 
buildings in a scheme (Section 60(4)(a) and (d)). A dispossessed owner is to be 
allotted a final plot (Section 65(2)(d)) and compensat ed for injurious affection 
(Section 105). Rule 21 does not require either to happen before eviction. 
• Contrary to the Constitution and to Parliament's protective laws.  Eviction must 
follow a procedure that is fair, just and reasonable (Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal 
Corporation, (1985) 3 SCC 545; Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India , (1978) 1 SCC 
248). Deprivation of property must comply with Article 300A ( K.T. Plantation v. 
State of Karnataka , (2011) 9 SCC 1). The Supreme Court has held that Article 
300A carrie s procedural rights to notice, a hearing, a reasoned decision, 
acquisition only for a public purpose, restitution or fair compensation, an efficient 
and expeditious procedure, and conclusion of the proceedings (Kolkata Municipal 
Corporation v. Bimal Kumar Shah, 2024). Wrongful dispossession of a Scheduled 
Tribe member from land without consent is an offence under Section 3(1)(f) and 
(g) of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) 
Act, 1989. Section 41 of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land 
Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 provides special 
safeguards for Scheduled Tribe families. The Pandaram judgment forbids eviction 
of the occupants it protects.

III. Finance, penalties, and the protective rules left out (Rules 26 to 31) 
28. Rules 27 to 30 – accounts kept from the public. Section 121 requires the budget to 
go "through the Chief Town P lanner (Administration)"; Rule 27 sends it directly to the 
Government. Section 122(1) requires an annual statement of accounts including a 
balance sheet; Rule 28 asks only for a "summary" of the Fund account. Nothing provides 
for the audited accounts and audit report (Section 122(4)) or the annual reports (Section 
123; Section 153(2)(lxxi) and (lxxii)) to be made public. Rule 29 applies superseded 
pension rules. Rule 30 offers certified copies only of "any order", while Section 130(2) 
concerns copies of plans and schemes. 
Amendment sought: route the budget as Section 121 requires; prescribe Section 
122 statement and the Section 123 report forms; publish audited accounts, audit 
reports and annual reports on the website; extend Rule 30 to plans and schemes; 
and give affected owners one free copy of every entry, plan and order concerning 
their land. 
29. Rule 31 – a vague penalty. Rule 31 penalises "any contravention made by any person 
on any of the Rules above", with a daily penalty "after conviction". Section 153(3) allows 
a penalty for a contravention of a rule, but a penalty clause must say what conduct it 
punishes. As drafted, an islander could be penalised for an incomplete Form B. 
"Conviction" is foreign to the Regulation, whose penalties are adjudicated under Section 
138 with notice, a written representation and a hearing, and are appealable under 
Section 139. 
Amendment sought: list the specific obligations whose breach is punishable; 
replace "after conviction" with the words of Section 153(3); and provide that  
penalties are imposed only by the adjudicating officer under Section 138. 
30. The protective rules the Regulation requires but the draft Rules omit.  The draft 
Rules prescribe in detail how to charge (Rule 24), penalise (Rule 31) and evict (Rule 
21). They leave out almost every procedure the Regulation requires to be prescribed for 
the protection of the people affected. Where a section says "within such period, and in 
such manner, as may be prescribed" and nothing is prescribed, an aggrieved person 
has no defined window, while Section 142 makes the Authority's orders "conclusive and 
enforceable". Selective rule-making of this kind is arbitrary under Article 14.

Effect on the islanders 
31. Taken together, the draft Rules produce the following result. The Authorities are 
controlled by the Administration, and the Gram Panchayat nominees can be removed 
or replaced at its will. Land is recorded in mainland categories that ignore  ownership 
and custom, and the record becomes conclusive once published. Every house, repair 
and re -roof attracts plans, fees and charges from which only Government land is 
exempt. Eviction can follow on an Authority's opinion after ten days' notice. On is lands 
of about 32 sq. km, where almost every family holds a small coconut plot and depends 
on the shore and lagoon, these provisions put homes, livelihoods and the rights declared 
by the Pandaram judgment at risk. 
The undersigned therefore requests that the Administration to: 
(a) keep the draft Rules in abeyance until the steps below are completed; 
(b) republish the draft Rules with Malayalam and Mahl translations, an explanatory 
note in simple language and an impact assessment, as the Pre -Legislative 
Consultation Policy, 2014 requires; allow at least 60 days from the publication of 
the translations; and accept objections by post or by hand at the offices of the 
Deputy Collectors, Block Development Officers and Gram Panchayats, in any 
language (Article 350); 
(c) consult the District Panchayat and all ten Village (Dweep) Panchayats through 
island-wise public hearings, and consult the National Commission for Scheduled 
Tribes under Article 338A(9); 
(d) amend the draft Rules as suggested , 
(e) frame and publish the protective rules listed above together with the present draft 
Rules; 
(f) insert a saving clause protecting occupants covered by the Pandaram judgment 
and the customary land rights of the Scheduled Tribes of Lakshadweep;

Summary of objections 
The draft Rules should not be notified in their present form. Several provisions exceed the rule-
making power in Section 153, contradict the sections they claim to implement, or are arbitrary 
under Article 14.  
(i) Consultation is defective.  The draft Rules were published only in English, with 
objections accepted only by email, and without the explanatory note and impact 
statement required by the Pre -Legislative Consultation Policy, 2014. The 
Regulation's own Sections 26(1)(h) and S.127 require people to be reached in local 
languages and by wide local publicity. 
(ii) The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes and the elected Panchayats  
were not consulted , though Article 338A(9) requires the Commission to be 
consulted on major policy matters affecting Scheduled Tribes. 
(iii) Rules 3 and 4 let the Government extend the term of, remove, and replace the only 
elected member of the Board and the tw o co -opted experts. Sections 5 and 
153(2)(ii) permit rules only on term, casual vacancies and allowances, and Section 
4(2)(o) and (q) say who chooses these members. 
(iv) Rule 6 gives the Chairperson the power to fix meetings, which Section 7 gives to 
the Board; allows decisions without a quorum after a ten -minute adjournment; 
exempts shareholders from the duty to disclose an interest; and keeps minutes in 
English, closed to the public. 
(v) No procedure for appeals. The Administrator presides, with a casting vote, over 
appeals against Authorities that his Administration controls, and the draft Rules 
provide no bench, recusal, place of hearing or time limit. 
(vi) Rule 7 sets a 90-day clock for approving the Regional Plan but prescribes nothing 
the Government must consider. 
Preliminary objections 
4. The consultation does not meet the Regulation's own standard. 
• Section 153(7) makes previous publication a condition of the rule -making power: 
in the Official Gazette, and "in such other manner as may be directed by the 
Government". The 30 days should run from the date of Gazette publication, and 
that date should be disclosed. 
• The Regulation requires community participation "by publishing reports in local 
languages so that community can understand and participate" (Section 26(1)(h)). It 
requires every public notice to be "widely made known in the locality" by affixing 
copies in public places, beat of drum or local newspaper (Section 127). The draft 
Rules were published only in English, and objections are accepted only by email.

• The Pre -Legislative Consultation Policy, 2014, adopted by the Committee of 
Secretaries on 10. 01.2014, applies to subordinate legislation. It requires a brief 
justification, the essential elements, the financial implications and an estimate of 
the impact on the environment, fundamental rights, and the lives and livelihoods 
of the people affected (paragraph 2). It requires publicity that reaches the affected 
group (paragraph 3) and an explanatory note in simple language (paragraph 5). 
None accompanied the draft Rules. 
• In Sajeev Sebastian v. Union of India (2024:KER:67376), the High Court of Kerala 
directed publication of a Malayalam translation of a draft notification and allowed 
60 days from that publication for objections. In Vikrant Tongad v. Union of India  
(W.P.(C) No. 3747 of 2020, Delhi High Court, 30.06.2020), the draft EIA 
Notification, 2020 w as directed to be translated into the languages of the Eighth 
Schedule. Transparency in making subordinate legislation was stressed in Cellular 
Operators Association of India v. TRAI, (2016) 7 SCC 703. 
• Article 350 entitles every person to submit a represen tation in any language used 
in the Union. An English, email -only process denies that in practice to most 
islanders, many of whom have no reliable internet access. 
5. The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes and the elected Panchayats were 
not consulted. About 95% of the people of Lakshadweep are Scheduled Tribes (Census 
2011). Rules that decide who sits on the body that plans the use of every island are "major 
policy matters affecting Scheduled Tribes", on which Article 338A(9) requires the 
Commission to b e consulted. Part IX of the Constitution applies to Union Territories 
(Article 243L); the Gram Panchayats are the "local authority" under Section 2(18); and 
land improvement, land consolidation and rural housing are Eleventh Schedule subjects 
(items 2 and 10). The District Panchayat, all ten Village (Dweep) Panchayats and the 
Commission should be consulted before the draft Rules are finalised. 
6. The ecological safeguards of the Regulation are given no effect.  The Board must 
ensure that plans do not exceed carrying capacity (Section 8(2)(j)). The Regional Plan it 
considers must rest on a coral study and demarcate sensitive areas, reefs, lagoons and 
fishing zones (Section 11(2)(e), (f), (g) and (i)). The Integrated Island Management Plan, 
prepared under the directions of the Supreme Court in Union Territory of Lakshadweep 
v. Seashells Beach Resort  (Civil Appeal Nos. 4625 –4626 of 2012), the Island Coastal 
Regulation Zone Notification, 2019 and the EIA Notification, 2006 also apply. The draft 
Rules give none of these requirements a step or a check in the Board's procedure (see the 
objection to Rule 7 below). 
7. Careless drafting.  The draft Rules invoke Section 153(2)(iv) (other functions of the 
Board) but contain no such rule, and Rule 7 rests on Section 153(2)(v), whi ch is not 
invoked. Rules that erroneously recite  the power under which they are made show non-
application of mind.

Objections to specific rules 
8. The Board has 21 members under Section 4(2). Eighteen are officials: fourteen officers 
of the Union Territory, led by the Administrator as Chairperson, three Central 
Government officers, and the Chief Town Planner. One member is elected by the District 
Panchayat (clause (o)), and two experts are co -opted (clause (q)). The draft Rules make 
even those three seats depend on the Administration's pleasure. 
9. Rule 3(1), first proviso – open-ended extension of term (ultra vires Sections 4(2)(o), 
4(2)(q) and 5).  The clause (o) member is "elected by the Members" of the District 
Panchayat, and the clause (q) experts are "co-opted by the Members specified in clauses 
(a) to (p)". Sections 5 and 153(2)(ii) allow the Rules to fix the term, the manner of filling 
casual vacancies and allowances. They do not allow the Government to decide who stays 
in a seat. Yet the proviso lets the G overnment extend a term "by such period or periods 
not exceeding one year at a time", without limit. The Government could keep an elected 
member in office after the District Panchayat wants someone else, or keep co -opted 
experts indefinitely. 
Amendment sought: a fixed term of two years, coextensive  with membership of the 
District Panchayat, with no extension; on expiry, a fresh election or co -option by the 
body that chose the member. 
10. Rule 3(2) – removal by the Government (no rule-making power; arbitrary). Sections 
5 and 153(2)(ii) confer no power to remove a member; Section 6 provides only for 
resignation. Rule 3(2) lets the Government remove a member on its "opinion" of 
misconduct or incompetence, or "for any other good and sufficient reason". The 
"Government" is the Administration headed by the Administrator (Section 2(12)), who 
also chairs the Board (Section 4(2)(a)). The one elected member could be removed by 
the Chairperson he may have to oppose. The Regulation itself gives a Town Planning 
Officer, an appointee, the protection of an inquiry, a statement of charges and a hearing 
before removal (Section 71(2), proviso). A rule giving an elected representative less 
protection than the Regulation provides  an appointed officer is arbitrary under Article 
14. 
Amendment sought: delete Rule 3(2). If a removal clause is retained, the clause (o) 
member should be removable only by resolution of the District Panchayat. The clause 
(q) experts should be removable only on stated grounds, after an inquiry by a retired 
judicial officer and with the approval of the members who co-opted them. 
11. Rule 3(3)(iv) read with Rule 6(2) – loss of seat for meetings never notified. A member 
ceases to hold office if absent from three consecutive meetings or for six months. Yet 
Rule 6(2) says non -receipt of the meeting notice does not invalidate the meeting. A 
member can lose the seat for missing meetings he was never told about, whic h is a real 
risk where inter-island travel stops in the monsoon.

Amendment sought: notice by registered post and email with proof of delivery; 
cessation only after notice and a hearing; absence for want of notice, or for suspension 
of sea or air transport, not to count. 
12. Rule 4 – casual vacancies filled by the Government (contrary to Section 4(2)(o) and 
(q)). A "manner of filling casual vacancies" under Section 5 must follow the manner in 
which the seat is filled under Section 4. The Regulation knows the dif ference: for 
Planning and Development Authorities, Section 25(3) fills vacancies "by fresh 
appointment by the Government, or by nomination by the local authority concerned, as 
the case may be". Rule 4 turns the elected seat and the co -opted seats into Government 
nominations. 
Amendment sought: a vacancy in the clause (o) seat to be filled by fresh election by the 
District Panchayat within 30 days, and a clause (q) vacancy by fresh co-option. 
13. Rule 6(1) – the Chairperson fixes meetings (contrary to Section 7).  Section 7 says 
"The Board shall meet at such times and places as it may think fit". Section 153(2)(iii) 
allows rules only on "rules of procedure in regard to the transaction of business". Rule 
6(1) moves the decision from the Board to the Chairperson. 
Amendment sought: the Board to fix its calendar by resolution; a special meeting on 
requisition by one-third of the members or by the District Panchayat member; meetings 
on matters concerning an island to be held on that island or with video participation 
from it. 
14. Rule 6(4) – decisions without a quorum. The quorum is half the members (11 of 21), 
but if it is not present, the meeting is adjourned for ten minutes and then held "not 
requiring a quorum". A meeting of the Administrator and a few officers could then  
recommend the Regional Plan (Sections 12 and 13), direct Master Plans and town 
planning schemes (Section 8(2)), and decide appeals under Sections 49, 56, 57, 58 and 
118. Under Section 143(2), once the minutes are signed, the meeting is "taken to have 
been duly convened and to be free from all defects". A ten-minute adjournment therefore 
removes the only guarantee that a collective body acts collectively. 
Amendment sought: an adjourned meeting to be held on a later day with fresh notice, 
with a quorum of at  least one-third required . For plans, schemes and appeals, the 
quorum should include the District Panchayat member or a co-opted expert. 
15. Rule 6(3) and 6(6) – the Administrator presides, with a casting vote, over appeals 
against his own Administration.  The Board hears appeals against Planning and 
Development Authorities (Sections 49, 56, 57, 58 and 118). The Government controls 
those Authorities (Section 145), approves the plans they implement (Sections 14 and 37), 
and decides acquisition notices (Section 51). The draft Rules prescribe no procedure for 
appeals: no bench, no recusal, no place of hearing, no time limit. 
Amendment sought: an Appeals Committee of the Board that includes the District 
Panchayat member and a co-opted expert, presided over by a memb er with no part in

the order under appeal; hearings on the appellant's island or by video; reasoned 
orders; and disposal within three months, failing which the appeal is deemed allowed 
as Section 49(3) already provides. 
16. Rule 6(7), proviso – shareholders not treated as interested. Planning bodies under the 
Regulation contract for land (Section 24(2)) and may sell up to 15% of land in a town 
planning scheme for residential, commercial or industrial use (Section 60(4)(k)(i)(D)). A 
member holding shares in a resort or real estate company concerned in a proposal is "not 
deemed" interested. 
Amendment sought: delete the proviso; require disclosure of any shareholding or 
directorship of the member or a relative in a company concerned; publish a register of 
members' interests. 
17. Rule 6(8) – minutes in English only, open only to members.  The Board's decisions 
shape land use for the whole Union Territory, yet its agenda and minutes will be in 
English and open to inspection only by members. 
Amendment sought: agenda and min utes in English and Malayalam (with a Mahl 
summary for Minicoy), published on the Administration's website and island notice 
boards within seven days, with reasons for each decision. 
18. Rule 7 – a 90 -day clock for the Regional Plan, and nothing on what must b e 
considered. The Regional Plan covers the whole Union Territory for a twenty -year 
horizon (Section 10(2)). Rule 7 gives the Government 90 days to decide on it, while the 
companion draft Planning and Development Authority Rules (Rules 13 and 15) give it 
180 days each to examine a Master Plan for a single planning area. No reason is given 
for this inversion. More importantly, Rule 7 sets only a clock. It prescribes nothing about 
what the Government must weigh before approving a plan that will bind every island. 
Amendment sought: a period of 180 days, consistent with the Master Plan rules; and 
a requirement that the approval under Section 14 record reasons. The reasons should 
show that the Government considered the public comments under Section 13, the coral 
study under Section 11(2)(e), conformity with the Integrated Island Management Plan 
and the Island Coastal Regulation Zone Notification, 2019, the carrying capacity 
study, the views of the District Panchayat and Gram Panchayats, and the views of the 
National Commission for Scheduled Tribes. 
19. Representation on the Board. The Rules cannot alter Section 4, but the Administration 
should recommend to the Central Government an amendment that adds the elected 
Chairpersons of the Village (Dweep) Panchayats and repres entatives of fishermen and 
women to the Board. Pending that, the co -option under clause (q) should include one 
expert in coral reef or atoll ecology and one person versed in the customary land tenure 
of the islands.

The undersigned therefore requests that the Administration: 
(a) keep the draft Rules in abeyance until the steps below are completed; 
(b) republish the draft Rules with Malayalam and Mahl translations, an explanatory 
note in simple language and an impact assessment, as the Pre -Legislative 
Consultation Policy, 2014 requires; allow at least 60 days from the publication of 
the translations; and  accept objections by post or by hand at the offices of the 
Deputy Collectors, Block Development Officers and Gram Panchayats, in any 
language (Article 350); 
(c) consult the District Panchayat and all ten Village (Dweep) Panchayats through 
island-wise public hearings, and consult the National Commission for Scheduled 
Tribes under Article 338A(9); 
(d) amend the draft Rules as suggested , 
(e) recommend to the Central Government an amendment of Section 4 to give the 
elected Village (Dweep) Panchayats, fishermen and women a place on the Board; 
(f) publish a summary of the objections received, with reasons for accepting or 
rejecting each; and

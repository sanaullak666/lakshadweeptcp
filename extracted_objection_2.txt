Summary of objections 
The draft Rules should not be notified in their present form.  
(i) Rule 3(b) is ultra vires Section 3.  It lets the Administrator relax every 
qualification and "appoint or nominate any person" as Chief Town Planner. 
(ii) Rule 3(b) is arbitrary  and bypasses Articles 14, 16 and 320(3): no criteria, no 
reasons, no selection, no limit of time. 
(iii) The essential qualifications ignore the island, coastal and ecological functions the 
Regulation gives the post. 
(iv) The draft Rules are incomplete. They prescribe no method of recruitment, tenure, 
age limit, pay, disqualification, or bar on conflicting interests. 
(v) No place for the Scheduled Tribes of the islands. There is no reservation or local 
preference, and no knowledge of Malayalam or Mahl is required. 
(vi) Consultation is defective, and the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes was 
not consulted. 
 Preliminary objections 
4. The consultation does not meet the Regulation's own standard. 
• Section 153(7) makes previous publication a condition of the rule -making power: 
in the Official Gazette, and "in such other manner as may be directed by the 
Government". The 30 days should run from the date of Gazette publication, and 
that date should be disclosed. 
• The Regulation requires community participation "by publishing reports in local 
languages so that community can understand and participate" (Section 26(1)(h)). It 
requires every public notice to be "widely made known in the locality" by affixing 
copies in public places, beat of drum or local newspaper (Section 127). The draft 
Rules were published only in English, and objections are accepted only by email. 
• The P re-Legislative Consultation Policy, 2014, adopted by the Committee of 
Secretaries on 10.01.2014, applies to subordinate legislation. It requires a brief 
justification, the essential elements, the financial implications and an estimate of 
the impact on the environment, fundamental rights, and the lives and livelihoods 
of the people affected (paragraph 2). It requires publicity that reaches the affected 
group (paragraph 3) and an explanatory note in simple language (paragraph 5). 
None accompanied the draft Rules. 
• In Sajeev Sebastian v. Union of India (2024:KER:67376), the High Court of Kerala 
directed publication of a Malayalam translation of a draft notification and allowed 
60 days from that publication for objections. In Vikrant Tongad v. Union of India  
(W.P.(C) No. 3747 of 2020, Delhi High Court, 30.06.2020), the draft EIA 
Notification, 2020 was directed to be translated into the languages of the Eighth

Schedule. Transparency in making subordinate legislation was stressed in Cellular 
Operators Association of India v. TRAI, (2016) 7 SCC 703. 
• Article 350 entitles every person to submit a representation in any language used 
in the Union. An English, email -only process denies that in practice to most 
islanders, many of whom have no reliable internet access. 
5. The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes and the elected Panchayats were 
not consulted. About 95% of the people of Lakshadweep are Scheduled Tribes (Census 
2011). Rules that decide the qualifications of the officer who will plan and decide land 
questions for a population that is overwhelmingly tribal, and whether any place is kept 
for local Scheduled Tribe candidates, are "major policy matters affecting Scheduled 
Tribes", on which Article 338A(9) requires the Commission to be consulted. Part IX of 
the Constitution applies to Union Territories (Article 243L); the Gram Panchayats are 
the "local authority" under Section 2(18); and land improvement, land consolidation and 
rural housing are Eleventh Schedule subjects (items 2 and 10). The District Panchayat, 
all ten Village (Dweep) Panchayats and the Commission should be consulted before the 
draft Rules are finalised. 
Objections to Rule 3 
6. Rule 3(b) is ultra vires Section 3.  Section 3 requires the Administrator to "appoint a 
Chief Town Planner possessing such qualifications in town and country planning, as may 
be prescribed". Section 153(2)(i) empowers the Rules to prescribe "the qualifications". 
Rule 3(b) instead empowers the Administrator "to relax any / all of the above 
qualifications / conditions and appoint or n ominate any person, for a specific period of 
time". A power to prescribe qualifications is not a power to dispense with them. Rule 
3(b) makes the words "possessing such qualifications in town and country planning" in 
Section 3 meaningless, and adds a power  to "nominate" where Section 3 requires 
appointment by notification. A rule cannot travel beyond, or be inconsistent with, the 
provision under which it is made . 
7. Rule 3(b) is arbitrary and violates Articles 14, 16 and 320(3).  The power has no 
criteria, needs no reasons in writing, is not confined to any class of cases, and requires 
no advertisement or selection. It extends to "any person". The "specific period" has no 
outer limit and can be renewed. Public posts must be filled through an open process 
consistent with Articles 14 and 16 . Article 320(3)(a) and (b) require the Union Public 
Service Commission to be consulted on methods of recruitment and on the principles for 
appointment to civil posts under the Union, and Rule 3(b) bypasses this. Even the model 
"power to relax" clause in Central recruitment rules is limited to relaxation for a class or 
category of persons, for reasons recorded in writing, in consultation with the 
Commission. 
8. The essential qualifications do not match the post's statutory function s. The 
Regulation requires the Regional Plan to rest on a coral study, to demarcate coastal 
regulation zones, sensitive areas, reefs, lagoons and fishing zones, and to plan for reef 
rejuvenation (Section 11(2)(e) to (i)). It requires the Master Plan to car ry the Coastal

Zone Management Plan or the Integrated Island Management Plan, and zoning fit for 
islands four to six metres above sea level (Section 33(1)(a)(ix) and (b)). Rule 3(a) asks 
only for a postgraduate qualification in town planning, seven years " in a responsible 
position" (an undefined term), and "knowledge of Town Planning laws and civic 
designs". It requires no experience of coastal, island or atoll planning, and no knowledge 
of the Island Coastal Regulation Zone Notification, 2019, the Integrat ed Island 
Management Plan prepared under the directions of the Supreme Court in Union Territory 
of Lakshadweep v. Seashells Beach Resort  (Civil Appeal Nos. 4625 –4626 of 2012), 
environmental law, sea -level rise or the land tenure of the islands. The "desira ble" 
qualifications (membership of the Institute of Town Planners, a degree in architecture or 
civil engineering) are generic to the mainland. 
9. The draft Rules are incomplete. They prescribe no method of recruitment, tenure, age 
limit, pay level, probation,  disqualification, or bar on private practice or on interests in 
land, tourism or construction in the Union Territory. The post is left almost entirely to 
discretion. 
10. No place for the Scheduled Tribes of the islands.  The draft Rules make no provision 
for reservation or local preference (Articles 16(4), 46 and 335), and require no knowledge 
of Malayalam or Mahl. A Chief Town Planner who cannot read the islanders' objections, 
or understand the customary tenure behind them, cannot discharge Sections 13 and 
26(1)(h). 
11. Rule 3 as it should read.  The Administration is requested to replace Rule 3 with the 
following: 
"3. Qualifications of Chief Town Planner. — (a) Essential: (i) a 
postgraduate degree in Regional, Urban or Town Planning from a recognised 
university; (ii) seven years' experience in town and country planning, of 
which at least three years in coastal, island or other ecologically fragile areas; 
(iii) working knowledge of the Island Coastal Regulation Zone Notification, 
2019, the Integrated Island Management Plan for Lakshadweep, the 
Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and the EIA Notification, 2006."  
"(b) Desirable: published work on atoll or small -island planning, coral reef 
conservation or climate adaptation; knowledge of Malayalam or Mahl and of 
the customary land tenure of the islands." 
"(c) Method of recruitment: by the method, and with the reservation and local 
preference for Scheduled Tribes of Lakshadweep, applicable to Group A 
posts in the Union Territory, in consultation with the Union Public Service 
Commission. Where filled by deputation, for not more than three years, with 
proficiency in Malayalam or Mahl to be acquired within the first year." 
"(d) Power to relax: the Administrator may, for reasons recorded in writing 
and in consultation with the Union Public Service Commission, relax the 
requirement of experience in clause (a)(ii) for a class or category of 
candidates, but no other essential qualification."
